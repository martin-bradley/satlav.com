What visitors to France should know

Are public toilets free?

Some public toilets in France are free to use, especially in larger cities, parks, shopping centres and civic spaces. Paris is a good example: the city has hundreds of public sanisettes, many of which are free automatic toilets.

Charges are still worth expecting in some places, particularly in busy tourist areas, privately managed toilets, some transport hubs and facilities with attendants. A contactless card or a small amount of change can still be useful when travelling.

Where can you usually find toilets?

Public toilets in France are often found in town and city centres, railway stations, parks, shopping centres, museums, galleries, motorway service areas and major tourist attractions.

Cafés, bars and restaurants usually have toilets for customers. Some businesses may allow non-customers to use them, but this varies, and it is polite to ask first.

Accessibility

Accessibility varies. Many modern public toilets and larger venues provide accessible facilities, but older buildings, smaller cafés and some public facilities may be more limited.

In Paris, many sanisettes are designed to be accessible, but access, space and reliability can still vary in practice. Sat Lav shows accessibility information where it is known. Unknown details are left as unknown rather than guessed.

Baby-changing and family facilities

Baby-changing facilities are common in larger shopping centres, department stores, museums, family attractions and motorway service areas. Smaller public toilets, cafés and older buildings may not always provide them.

Opening hours

Opening hours vary widely. Toilets inside buildings usually follow the opening hours of that building, while standalone public toilets may close in the evening or during cleaning. Automated toilets may also be temporarily unavailable while a cleaning cycle runs.

Practical tips

  • In Paris, look for public sanisettes, parks, museums, department stores and larger transport hubs.
  • Let automated toilets complete their cleaning cycle before trying to enter.
  • If a café or bar toilet is not clearly public, assume it is for customers unless staff say otherwise.
  • Motorway service areas and rest areas are useful toilet stops on longer journeys.
  • Keep a contactless card or small amount of change available for paid facilities.
  • Opening information is not always published, so it is sensible to check more than one nearby option when possible.

Common questions

Are public toilets free in France?

Some are free, including Paris sanisettes. Others may charge a small fee, particularly in tourist areas, transport hubs or privately managed facilities.

Can I use toilets in cafés and restaurants?

Usually yes if you are a customer. Some cafés, bars and restaurants allow non-customers to use their toilets, but this varies. If in doubt, it is polite to ask first.

What are Paris sanisettes?

Sanisettes are automated public toilets found around Paris. They are self-cleaning and usually free to use, although they may close briefly while cleaning takes place.

Do I need cash?

Not always. Many toilets are free, and many paid facilities now accept contactless payment. A small amount of change can still be useful when travelling.

Are toilets available on French motorways?

Yes. Motorway service areas and many rest areas provide toilets. Larger staffed service areas usually have more facilities than smaller rest areas.

What if a toilet is closed?

Opening hours can vary, and some toilets may close temporarily for cleaning, maintenance or local reasons. Sat Lav displays known opening information and allows the community to report problems where supported.
