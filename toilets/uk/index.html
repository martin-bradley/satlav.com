What visitors to the UK should know

Are public toilets free?

Many public toilets in the UK are free to use, especially those in shopping centres, museums, galleries, libraries, larger supermarkets and some council-run facilities. A smaller number of toilets charge a modest fee, particularly in transport hubs, tourist areas or privately managed locations.

Compared with some European countries, paying to use a toilet is not usually the default expectation across most of the UK, but it is still worth carrying a payment card or a small amount of change when travelling.

Where can you usually find toilets?

Public toilets in the UK are often found in town and city centres, railway stations, shopping centres, public parks, libraries, leisure centres, museums, galleries, service stations and major tourist attractions.

Pubs, cafés and restaurants usually have toilets for customers. Some businesses may allow non-customers to use them, but this varies, and it is polite to ask first.

Accessibility and RADAR keys

Many UK toilets include accessible cubicles, although the quality, space and reliability of access can vary. Some accessible toilets are kept locked and require a RADAR key, part of a national scheme that helps disabled people access locked accessible facilities.

Sat Lav shows accessibility information and RADAR key requirements where they are known. Unknown details are left as unknown rather than guessed.

Baby changing and family facilities

Baby-changing facilities are common in larger shopping centres, department stores, museums, family attractions and motorway service areas. Smaller public toilets may not always provide them.

Opening hours

Opening hours vary widely. Toilets inside buildings usually follow the opening hours of that building, while standalone public toilets may close in the evening. Motorway service areas and some larger transport hubs often provide toilets around the clock.

Practical tips

  • Large cities usually have more options, but the nearest toilet may be inside a station, shopping centre, museum or department store rather than a standalone public toilet.
  • Smaller towns may rely more on libraries, community buildings, parks, supermarkets and council facilities.
  • Motorway service areas almost always have toilets and are generally free to use.
  • Department stores and larger supermarkets are often useful places to look for customer toilets.
  • If a toilet is inside a pub, café or restaurant, it is usually intended for customers.
  • Opening information is not always published, so it is sensible to check more than one nearby option when possible.

Common questions

Are public toilets free in the UK?

Many are free, especially in shopping centres, museums, galleries, libraries, larger supermarkets and some council-run facilities. Some toilets charge a modest fee, especially in transport hubs, tourist areas or privately managed locations.

Can I use toilets in pubs and cafés?

Usually yes if you are a customer. Some pubs, cafés and restaurants allow non-customers to use their toilets, but this varies. If in doubt, it is polite to ask first.

Do I need cash to use toilets?

Most public toilets are free. Where a charge applies, contactless payment is increasingly common, but it can still be useful to carry a small amount of change when travelling.

Are there toilets at UK train stations?

Many larger railway stations have toilets, although availability, charges and opening hours vary. Smaller stations may not have public toilets.

What is a RADAR key?

A RADAR key is used by many disabled people in the UK to access locked accessible toilets. Sat Lav shows RADAR key requirements where this information is known.

What if a toilet is closed?

Opening hours can vary, and some toilets may close temporarily for cleaning, maintenance or local reasons. Sat Lav displays known opening information and allows the community to report problems where supported.
